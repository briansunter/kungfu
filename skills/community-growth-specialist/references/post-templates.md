# Community Post Templates and Structures

---

## The 5-Part Post Framework

All high-engagement posts follow this structure:

1. **Hook with personal struggle** (1-2 sentences)
2. **Pivot point** ("Then I discovered...")
3. **Numbered tactics with specific results**
4. **Honest admission of failure**
5. **Call-to-action as question**

---

## Template 1: Journey/Milestone Post ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Use for**: Revenue milestones, product launches, progress updates
**Engagement**: 30-50+ comments **Conversion**: 18-23%

### Structure

```
[Headline: Specific numbers + timeframe]
"$10K MRR in 5 months: Here's how I did it (and what I'd do differently)"

[Hook with personal struggle]
"I spent 6 months building something nobody wanted.
Hit $0 MRR for months straight. Was ready to quit."

[Pivot point]
"Then I had one conversation that changed everything.
A founder told me: 'You're solving the wrong problem.'"

[The shift]
"I went back to the drawing board.
This time, I listened to customers first."

[Numbered tactics with specific results]
1. Posted daily to Indie Hackers for 90 days → 1,200 email signups
2. Cold emailed 50 agencies → 12 demo requests, 4 closed
3. Launched tiered pricing ($29/$99/$299) → ARPU up 74%
4. Implemented onboarding checklist → Activation up 25%
5. Started weekly journey posts → Community of 500+ founders

[Specific results]
- Month 1: $0 MRR, 0 customers
- Month 3: $2,000 MRR, 24 customers
- Month 5: $10,200 MRR, 87 customers
- Churn: 4.2% monthly
- ARPU: $117

[Honest admission of failure]
"What didn't work:
- Product Hunt launch (#7 Product of Day, 3 customers)
- Facebook ads ($500 spent, 0 customers)
- Building 10 features nobody used
- Spending 3 months on 'perfect' MVP"

[Call-to-action as question]
"Has anyone else struggled with finding product-market fit?
What finally worked for you?"

[Optional: Soft CTA]
"If you're building B2B SaaS, happy to share more details.
DM me or check my journey posts for the full breakdown."
```

### What Makes This Work

**Specific numbers** (not vague "growth"):

- ✅ "$10,200 MRR, 87 customers"
- ❌ "Significant revenue growth"

**Failure transparency** (builds trust):

- ✅ "Product Hunt launch...3 customers"
- ❌ Only sharing wins

**Actionable tactics** (not just inspiration):

- ✅ "Posted daily to IH for 90 days"
- ❌ "I engaged with the community"

**Question at the end** (drives engagement):

- ✅ "What worked for you?"
- ❌ "Follow my journey!"

## Template 2: Post-Mortem with Failure Transparency ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Use for**: Analyzing what didn't work, lessons learned **Engagement**: 40-60+
comments (highest of all formats) **Conversion**: 20-25%

### Structure

```
[Headline: Honest failure framing]
"Why my Product Hunt launch failed (and what I'd do differently)"

[The setup]
"I spent 6 weeks preparing for my Product Hunt launch.
Hired a launch specialist. Mobilized my 7,000-person email list.
Woke up at 5am to post at the optimal time.

Result: #7 Product of the Day.
340 upvotes.
89 signups.
3 paying customers."

[What went wrong (numbered analysis)]
1. **Wrong audience for PH**
   - Product: B2B tool for agencies
   - PH audience: Consumer-focused browsers
   - Mismatch: Innovation tourists vs qualified buyers

2. **Over-invested in single day event**
   - Spent 50% of marketing budget on launch day
   - Should have invested in 6-month community building
   - Launch events don't build sustainable businesses

3. **No community momentum**
   - Hadn't built Indie Hackers presence
   - No Reddit engagement
   - Launched to cold audience

4. **Product wasn't ready for mass market**
   - Still figuring out positioning
   - Onboarding wasn't smooth
   - Churned 40% of first 10 customers

[What I'd do differently]
"If I could start over:

1. **Spend 6 months on Indie Hackers first**
   - Journey posts weekly
   - Engage daily with other founders
   - Build 500-1,000 engaged followers
   - THEN launch on PH

2. **Skip the launch specialist**
   - $2K could fund 4 months of Reddit ads
   - Or 10 months of Buffer for social media
   - Community > launch optimization

3. **Fix product before launch**
   - Get first 10 customers manually
   - Reduce churn to <5%
   - Prove people stick around
   - Then amplify"

[The surprising outcome]
"Here's what's funny:
2 months later, I posted my PH failure on Reddit.
That post drove 1,450 visitors.
More than my actual #7 Product of Day launch (~800 visitors).

The failure post outperformed the launch."

[Key lessons]
- Launch events are overrated
- Community building is underrated
- Transparency builds more trust than success
- Share failures openly, people respect it

[Call-to-action]
"What's your worst launch experience?
What did you learn from it?"
```

### Why This Works

**Vulnerability creates connection**:

- People relate to struggle more than success
- "Me too" responses drive engagement
- Builds credibility (you're not selling a dream)

**Surprising outcome** (meta-strategy):

- Strategy post about PH outperformed PH itself
- "Infinite Marketing Glitch" effect
- People love "how I learned X" content

**Actionable lessons**:

- Not just "I failed"
- "Here's what I'd do differently"
- Others can apply these lessons

## Template 3: Tactical Playbook ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Use for**: Sharing specific frameworks, processes, systems **Engagement**:
15-25 comments **Conversion**: 15-18%

### Structure

```
[Headline: Problem + solution frame]
"How I validated my SaaS idea before writing a single line of code"

[The problem]
"Struggling with:
- How do I know if anyone wants this?
- Should I build it or test it first?
- What if I spend 6 months building something nobody wants?

I wrestled with these questions for months.
Then I discovered a 2-hour validation framework."

[The framework (numbered steps)]
Step 1: Reddit Signal Assessment (30 min)
Search r/SaaS for "friction complaints"
- Look for: "I hate doing X manually"
- Green light: 3+ recent posts about same problem
- Red flag: Zero mentions of the problem

Step 2: Indie Hackers Signal (30 min)
Monitor for "we built this internally" posts
- If 3+ founders built similar tools: Green light
- If zero mentions: Keep searching

Step 3: Fake Door Test (1 hour)
Create landing page describing solution
- Include pricing (test willingness to pay)
- Share in target communities
- Measure: 5%+ conversion = build, <2% = pivot

[Real example]
"I used this for my agency reporting tool:

Step 1: Found 47 posts in r/advertising about manual reporting hell
Step 2: 3 founders on IH mentioned building internal tools for this
Step 3: Fake door got 127 emails, 12 pre-orders at $49/month

Result: Built it, launched 6 weeks later, hit $5K MRR in month 3"

[Why this works]
- Tests demand before investing time
- Forces you to talk to customers
- Pricing included (tests willingness to pay)
- Fast iteration (fail fast, not fail slow)

[Common mistakes to avoid]
❌ Asking friends/family (not your market)
❌ Asking "Would you buy this?" (people lie)
❌ Skipping pricing (test commitment, not curiosity)
❌ Building in secret (no feedback loop)

[Action items for today]
1. Identify your target customer's community
2. Spend 30 minutes searching for pain signals
3. Create landing page (use Carrd, takes 1 hour)
4. Share and collect emails

[CTA]
"What validation methods have worked for you?
Or what failed spectacularly?"
```

### Why This Works

**Specific framework** (not vague advice):

- Clear steps anyone can follow
- Time estimates for each step
- Success/failure criteria

**Real example** (proof it works):

- Shows application of framework
- Specific results (127 emails, 12 pre-orders)
- Makes advice credible

**Actionable** (do today):

- "What you can do right now"
- Not just theory
- Implementable immediately

## Template 4: Technical Deep-Dive ⭐⭐⭐

**Use for**: Technical architectures, build processes, technical decisions
**Engagement**: 10-15 comments **Conversion**: 10-12%

### Structure

````
[Headline: Technical + outcome]
"How we scaled from 0 to 10K users with a $50/month infrastructure"

[The challenge]
"Wanted to build SaaS that could:
- Handle 10K users on day 1
- Cost <$50/month initially
- Scale to 100K users without rewrite
- Stay manageable as solo founder"

[The stack we chose]
Frontend: Next.js + Tailwind CSS
Backend: Supabase (PostgreSQL + Auth + Storage)
CDN: Cloudflare (free tier)
Email: Resend (100 emails/day free)
Monitoring: Sentry (free tier)
Payments: Stripe

[Why this stack]
1. **Next.js**: SSR for SEO, React for UX, Vercel for hosting
2. **Supabase**: PostgreSQL power, Auth built-in, real-time features
3. **Cloudflare**: Free CDN, DDoS protection, Workers for edge
4. **Resend**: 10x cheaper than SendGrid, better DX
5. **Sentry**: Error tracking, performance monitoring

[Architecture decisions]
Decision 1: Serverless vs dedicated server
→ Chose serverless (Vercel) for:
   - Zero devops overhead
   - Auto-scaling
   - Pay only for usage
   - Solo founder friendly

Decision 2: SQL vs NoSQL
→ Chose PostgreSQL (Supabase) for:
   - Relational data structure
   - ACID compliance
   - Mature tooling
   - Can migrate off Supabase later

Decision 3: Build vs buy for auth
→ Chose Supabase Auth (buy) for:
   - OAuth providers built-in
   - Row-level security
   - Not reinventing the wheel
   - Focus on product, not infrastructure

[Cost breakdown at 10K users]
- Vercel Pro: $20/month
- Supabase Pro: $25/month
- Cloudflare: Free
- Resend: $5/month
- Stripe: 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction
- **Total: $50/month fixed + transaction fees**

[Performance metrics]
- Avg page load: 1.2s
- API response: 150ms
- Uptime: 99.95% (Vercel + Supabase)
- CDN cache hit rate: 85%

[What we'd change]
Nothing so far. Stack has scaled beautifully.
At 100K users, would consider:
- Move to dedicated database ($200/month)
- Implement Redis caching ($50/month)
- Upgrade Vercel plan ($400/month)

on my_table for select

[CTA] "What's your stack? What would you change? Any tools you'd recommend
avoiding?"

## Comment Response Templates

### For "Congrats!" Comments

```

"Thanks @[Name]!

A lot of trial and error along the way. Biggest breakthrough was [specific
tactic]. Happy to share details if you're building something similar.

```

### For "How did you do X?" Comments

```

"Great question @[Name]!

Here's what worked for me:

[Specific steps 1-3]

Key insight: [Lesson learned]

Hope that helps! Let me know if you want more details."

```

### For "I tried that but it didn't work" Comments

```

"Totally hear you @[Name]!

I had the same experience initially. What changed for me: [specific pivot]

Different things work for different markets. What are you building? Maybe I can
share specific context."

```

### For Skeptical/Critical Comments

```

"Fair point @[Name]!

You're right that [acknowledge validity]. In my case, [explain why it worked for
you].

Curious: What's worked better for you? Always looking to learn from others'
experience."

```

## Post Frequency Guidelines

### Indie Hackers

**Minimum**: 1 post/week
**Optimal**: 2-3 posts/week
**Maximum**: Daily (if high-quality, not spammy)

**Timing**:
- Best: Tuesday-Thursday, 8am-11am PT
- OK: Friday, 8am-10am PT
- Avoid: Saturday-Sunday (low engagement)

### Reddit

**Per subreddit**: 1-2 posts/week max
**Engagement**: 5-10 comments/day on other posts (before posting yourself)

**Timing**:
- Best: Tuesday-Thursday, 6am-9am PT (catches US workday)
- OK: Monday, Wednesday, Friday mornings
- Avoid: Weekends

### General Rule

**Quality > Quantity**:
- One amazing post/week > 5 mediocre posts
- Engagement ratio matters more than post count
- If comments drop off, improve quality, don't increase frequency

---

**Remember**: Templates are frameworks, not scripts. Adapt them to your voice, your journey, your specific context. Authenticity beats polish every time.
