# Fake Door Testing Templates and Frameworks

Use fake-door tests to validate demand before building a full product.

## What a Fake Door Test Is

A fake-door test presents a clear offer (problem, solution, price, CTA) before
the product is fully built. It is a demand test, not a brand exercise.

## Required Components

- One focused audience segment.
- One clear problem statement.
- One concrete offer and starting price.
- One primary CTA (waitlist, pilot request, or demo request).
- One follow-up flow for responders.

## Landing Page Template

```markdown
[Headline] Help [audience] achieve [outcome] without [pain].

[Subheadline] [Product] is for [audience] who need [core job-to-be-done].

[Problem] Today, teams handle [task] with [current workaround], which causes
[cost].

[Solution] [Product] provides [key capability 1], [key capability 2], and [key
capability 3].

[Pricing] Early access starts at [$X/month].

[CTA] [Join waitlist] / [Request pilot] / [Book intro call]

[Qualifier Question] What is the biggest blocker in your current process?
```

## CTA and Qualification Patterns

Pick one CTA and keep it consistent during each test round.

- `Join waitlist`: best for broad discovery.
- `Request pilot`: best for B2B validation.
- `Book intro call`: best for high-ticket or complex workflows.

Add one qualifier field to separate curiosity from real intent.

## Message Testing Framework

Test one variable at a time:

1. Headline angle: problem-first vs outcome-first.
2. Offer framing: monthly price shown vs hidden until follow-up.
3. CTA wording: waitlist vs pilot vs call.
4. Proof style: workflow screenshot vs short testimonial.

### Test Log Template

```markdown
Test ID: T-01 Variable: Headline angle Control: Problem-first Variant:
Outcome-first Audience source: [channel] Traffic volume: [N] Primary metric:
[conversion to CTA] Secondary metric: [qualified replies] Outcome:
[Control/Variant/No clear winner] Decision: [Ship / Re-test / Discard]
```

## Traffic Strategy

### Organic Channels

- Niche communities where your users already discuss the problem.
- Founder communities for early B2B conversations.
- Personal network messages to people who match your ICP.

### Paid Channels

Use only after message-market fit signals exist. Keep budget small and compare
cost per qualified lead, not raw clicks.

## Decision Thresholds

Proceed to MVP when:

- You consistently receive qualified intent from your ICP.
- Feedback converges on a narrow core workflow.
- Pricing conversations indicate viable economics.

Pivot when:

- Traffic is high but qualified intent is weak.
- Interest is broad but non-committal.
- Users disagree on core problem priority.

Stop when:

- Repeated iterations fail to produce quality conversations.
- The problem is acknowledged but not urgent enough to pay for.

## Follow-Up Templates

### Waitlist Confirmation

```text
Subject: You’re on the early access list

Thanks for joining.
Quick question so we build the right thing first:
What’s your current workaround for [problem]?
```

### Pilot Invite

```text
Subject: Pilot slot for [product]

You mentioned [pain point].
We’re opening a small pilot for teams with that workflow.
Reply with team size and current stack if you want a slot.
```

## Common Failure Modes

- Testing multiple messages at once.
- Optimizing for clickthrough instead of qualified intent.
- Hiding pricing in early tests.
- Collecting emails with no follow-up workflow.
- Treating fake-door tests as a one-time event.
