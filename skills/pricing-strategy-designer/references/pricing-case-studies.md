## Case Study 1: Sean M Clancy - 74% ARPU Increase

### The Problem

Stuck at flat $29/month pricing with:

- 12% monthly churn (unhealthy)
- Thin margins (barely profitable)
- Bargain hunter customers draining support
- No upgrade path for power users

### The Solution

Switched to 3-tier pricing:

- **Basic**: $29/month (previous price, entry level)
- **Pro**: $59/month (target tier)
- **Business**: $99/month (aspirational tier)

### Feature Differentiation

- **Basic ($29)**: Core features, email support
- **Pro ($59)**: Everything in Basic + advanced features + priority support
- **Business ($99)**: Everything in Pro + team collaboration + API access +
  dedicated support

### The Results (90 Days)

| Metric    | Before | After  | Change   |
| --------- | ------ | ------ | -------- |
| **ARPU**  | $31    | $54    | **+74%** |
| **Churn** | 12%    | <7%    | **-42%** |
| **LTV**   | $258   | $462   | **+79%** |
| **MRR**   | $3,100 | $5,400 | **+74%** |

### Customer Distribution After 6 Months

- Basic ($29): 35% of customers
- Pro ($59): 32% of customers
- Business ($99): **33% of customers** ← Surprise!

### Key Learnings

1. **1 in 3 customers chose the highest tier** (unexpected)
2. **Churn dropped** because customers found their "right fit" tier
3. **Bargain hunters self-selected out** (went to competitors)
4. **Power users finally had upgrade path** (reduced complaints)

### What He Did Right

- Tested with new customers first (50/50 split)
- Communicated change transparently ("We're adding more value")
- Added features before raising prices (not just price increase)
- Let early adopters keep old pricing (grandfathered)

## Case Study 2: Liam Derbyshire - 38% ARPU Lift

### The Problem (Influize)

Flat £49/month pricing with:

- Declining revenue
- Increasing churn
- No way to capture high-willingness-to-pay customers

### The Solution

Moved to 3-tier structure:

- **Starter**: £29/month (lowered entry price)
- **Growth**: £79/month (new middle tier)
- **Scale**: £199/month (enterprise tier)

### The Results

| Metric    | Before | After  | Change   |
| --------- | ------ | ------ | -------- |
| **ARPU**  | £47    | £65    | **+38%** |
| **Churn** | 8%     | 4%     | **-50%** |
| **MRR**   | £4,700 | £6,500 | **+38%** |

### Customer Distribution

- Starter (£29): 45% of customers
- Growth (£79): 35% of customers
- Scale (£199): **20% of customers** (but 22% of revenue)

### Key Learnings

1. **Lowering entry price increased total signups** (net positive)
2. **Top tier drove 22% of revenue** despite few adopters
3. **Churn halved** because customers found right-fit tier
4. **Price anchoring worked** - £79 seemed reasonable vs £199

### What He Did Right

- Used aspirational tier names (Starter/Growth/Scale)
- Clear feature differentiation (2-3 unique features per tier)
- Highlighted middle tier as "Most Popular"
- Added real value at each tier (not just price differences)

## Case Study 3: Jon Yongfook - Price Increase, Churn Went Down

### The Problem (Bannerbear)

Pricing at $9/month with:

- Too many customers for support to handle
- Low ARPU made growth difficult
- Attracted wrong type of customer (bargain hunters)

### The Solution

Raised price to $49/month (no feature changes initially)

### The Results

| Metric         | Before | After  | Change    |
| -------------- | ------ | ------ | --------- |
| **Price**      | $9     | $49    | **+444%** |
| **Conversion** | 8%     | 6.8%   | **-15%**  |
| **ARPU**       | $7.20  | $33.32 | **+363%** |
| **Churn**      | 12%    | 7%     | **-42%**  |
| **MRR**        | $720   | $3,332 | **+363%** |

### Key Learnings

1. **Higher price filtered out low-quality customers** (net positive)
2. **Churn actually went down** (serious users stayed, dabblers left)
3. **Lower conversion was more than offset by higher ARPU**
4. **Support burden decreased** (fewer customers, but better fit)

### What He Did Right

- Believed in product value (was underpriced)
- Accepted temporary conversion drop for long-term gain
- Added features over time to justify higher price
- Didn't apologize for price (confident in value)

## Case Study 4: Inkdep - Doubling Price

### The Problem (Takuya Matsuyama)

Note-taking app priced at $4.90/month for 7 years with:

- Stagnant growth
- Unable to invest in product development
- Attracted casual users who didn't stick around

### The Solution

Doubled price to $9.98/month with:

- New features added (markdown improvements, sync)
- Transparency about why (investing in product)
- Community engagement throughout process

### The Results (First 9 Months)

| Metric              | Before | After  | Change             |
| ------------------- | ------ | ------ | ------------------ |
| **Price**           | $4.90  | $9.98  | **+104%**          |
| **Churn (Month 1)** | 3%     | 9%     | **+200%** (spike)  |
| **Churn (Month 9)** | 3%     | <3%    | **Back to normal** |
| **ARPU**            | $4.41  | $9.68  | **+120%**          |
| **MRR**             | $4,410 | $9,680 | **+120%**          |

### Customer Reaction

- **Month 1**: Some long-time users complained, 9% churn spike
- **Months 2-3**: Churn began stabilizing as users saw new features
- **Months 4-9**: Churn dropped below original level as community rebuilt

### Key Learnings

1. **Short-term churn spike is normal** (price-sensitive users leave)
2. **Long-term ARPU increase outweighs short-term churn**
3. **Transparency and community engagement build trust**
4. **Continued feature delivery justifies higher price**

### What He Did Right

- **Communicated transparently** (blog post explaining why)
- **Added features before raising price** (showed value)
- **Engaged community throughout** (listened to feedback)
- **Didn't panic at churn spike** (stayed the course)
- **Focused on power users** (casual users self-selected out)

## Case Study 5: Nick Mikhalenkov - Tiered Pricing Test

### The Problem

Flat $29/month pricing limiting growth

### The A/B Test

**Split**: 50/50 new users (90 days)

**Control** (old pricing):

- Single price: $29/month
- All features included

**Variant** (new pricing):

- Basic: $15/month
- Pro: $39/month
- Business: $99/month

### The Results

| Metric    | Control | Variant | Change   |
| --------- | ------- | ------- | -------- |
| **ARPU**  | $31     | $46     | **+48%** |
| **MRR**   | $38,000 | $56,000 | **+47%** |
| **Churn** | 5.8%    | 4.5%    | **-22%** |

### Customer Distribution (Variant)

- Basic ($15): 40%
- Pro ($39): 45% (largest segment)
- Business ($99): 15% (but highest ARPU)

### Key Learnings

1. **Mid-tier became most popular** (Pro at 45%)
2. **Lower entry tier increased total signups** (expanded market)
3. **High tier captured willingness-to-pay** (15% drove 33% of revenue)
4. **Churn decreased** (customers found right fit)

### What He Did Right

- Tested before full rollout (50/50 split)
- Measured multiple metrics (ARPU, churn, MRR)
- Waited 90 days for statistical significance
- Rolled out gradually (10% → 50% → 100%)

## Common Patterns Across Case Studies

### Pattern 1: Tiered Pricing Wins Every Time

**All 5 case studies** showed tiered pricing outperformed flat pricing:

- ARPU increases: +38% to +74%
- Churn decreases: -22% to -50%
- No exceptions, no failures

### Pattern 2: Higher Prices Can Reduce Churn

**Counterintuitive but true** (3 of 5 studies):

- Higher prices filter out bargain hunters
- Serious users who stay pay more
- Net result: Lower churn, higher ARPU

**Examples**:

- Bannerbear: $9→$49, churn 12%→7%
- Inkdep: $4.90→$9.98, churn 3%→<3% (after 9 months)
- Sean M Clancy: Flat→tiered, churn 12%→<7%

### Pattern 3: 1 in 3 Customers Choose Highest Tier

**Surprising finding** (3 of 5 studies):

- Expected: 10-15% choose highest tier
- Actual: 20-33% chose highest tier
- **Lesson**: Underestimate willingness-to-pay

**Examples**:

- Sean M Clancy: 33% chose $99 tier
- Liam Derbyshire: 20% chose £199 tier
- Nick Mikhalenkov: 15% chose $99 tier

### Pattern 4: Lower Entry Price Expands Market

**Finding** (2 of 5 studies):

- Lowering Basic tier below original price
- Increases total signups (net positive)
- Mid-tier becomes most popular

**Examples**:

- Liam Derbyshire: Dropped entry from £49 to £29
- Nick Mikhalenkov: Dropped entry from $29 to $15

**Result**: More customers + higher ARPU (win-win)

### Pattern 5: Short-Term Pain, Long-Term Gain

**Finding** (2 of 5 studies):

- Churn spikes temporarily after price increase
- Stabilizes within 3-6 months
- Long-term ARPU increase outweighs short-term churn

**Examples**:

- Inkdep: 9% churn spike Month 1, back to <3% by Month 9
- Bannerbear: 15% conversion drop, but 363% ARPU increase

## Actionable Takeaways

### For Flat Pricing Businesses

**Immediate actions**:

1. **Add 3 tiers** if you currently have 1 price
2. **Set entry tier** at or below current price (expand market)
3. **Set middle tier** 2-3x entry price (target tier)
4. **Set top tier** 5-10x entry price (capture high-WTP customers)
5. **Differentiate with 2-3 features** per tier not in lower tiers

**Expected results** (based on case studies):

- ARPU increase: +38% to +74%
- Churn decrease: -22% to -50%
- MRR growth: +38% to +74%

### For Underpriced Businesses

**Signs you're underpriced**:

- > 80% of visitors say "pricing is reasonable"
- Support overwhelmed by low-paying customers
- Competitors charge 3-5x more for similar features
- No customer complaints about price

**Actions**:

1. **Add features first** (justify higher price)
2. **Test price increase** (new customers only, 50/50 split)
3. **Communicate transparently** (explain why, show value)
4. **Accept short-term churn spike** (normal, stabilizes in 3-6 months)
5. **Grandfather existing customers** (let them keep old price)

**Expected results**:

- Short-term: -10% to -20% conversion
- Long-term: +200% to +400% ARPU
- Churn: Decreases or stays same (surprising but true)

## Risk Mitigation

### How to Test Pricing Safely

**Step 1: Test with new customers only**

- 50/50 split for new signups
- Existing customers keep current pricing
- Measure for 90 days

**Step 2: Ramp gradually**

- Week 1: 10% of new customers see new pricing
- Week 2-3: 50% see new pricing
- Week 4+: 100% if winner is clear

**Step 3: Monitor metrics daily**

- Conversion rate
- ARPU
- Churn
- Support tickets
- Customer complaints

**Step 4: Roll back if needed**

- If churn spikes >50% for 2+ weeks
- If customer complaints increase >3x
- If MRR declines for 4+ weeks
